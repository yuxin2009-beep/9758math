Integration by Parts (Derivation, LIATE, Standalone & Cyclic)

Derive the formula from the Product Rule, choose u via LIATE, and evaluate standard, standalone, and cyclic indefinite integrals

Derivation from Product Rule & LIATE Choice Hierarchy Choose u Earliest in LIATE (Differentiate u • Integrate dv/dx)
1. Product Rule ddx(uv) = u dvdx + v dudx
2. Integrate wrt x uv = ∫ u dvdx dx + ∫ v dudx dx
3. Rearrange (MF27):   ∫ u dvdx dx = uv − ∫ v dudx dx
L Logarithmic
ln x
I Inverse Trig
sin−1 x, tan−1 x
A Algebraic
x, x², xn
T Trigonometric
sin x, cos x, sec² x
E Exponential
eax, ex
Why LIATE works: Functions on the left (L, I) simplify dramatically when differentiated (losing their log/inverse-trig form), whereas functions on the right (T, E) stay equally easy when integrated.
Tab 1: LIATE Selector & Step-by-Step Integration by Parts
LIATE Strategy & Repeated-Pass Insights
How to Check Whether Your Choice of u Was Right:
Look at the new integral ∫ v dudx dx generated after Step 1:
• If the power of x dropped (e.g., x² → x → 1), you are on the right track.
• If the power of x increased (e.g., choosing dvdx = x ⇒ v = 12x² alongside u = e2x), swap u and dvdx immediately!
Bracket Discipline on Pass 2:
When applying Integration by Parts a second time (such as ∫ x² sin x dx), always wrap the second uv − ∫ v dudx dx inside square brackets [ … ] so minus signs distribute accurately.
Tab 2: Standalone ln x & Inverse Trig via Implicit dvdx = 1
Why "Multiply by 1" Unlocks L & I Functions
1. Neither ln x nor sin−1 x / tan−1 x is in MF27 directly:
Both sit at the very top of LIATE (L and I) because their derivatives are clean algebraic functions:
• ddx(ln x) = 1x
• ddx(tan−1 x) = 11 + x²
• ddx(sin−1 x) = 11 − x²
2. Treat 1 as the Algebraic Factor (dvdx = 1 ⇒ v = x):
Rewriting ∫ f(x) dx = ∫ f(x) · 1 dx converts the leftover integral ∫ v dudx dx into an algebraic fraction ∫ x1 + x² dx or ∫ x1 − x² dx that integrates immediately!
Tab 3: Cyclic Integration by Parts (I = ∫ eax sin bx dx & ∫ eax cos bx dx)
Golden Rules for Cyclic ("Boomerang") Integrals
Rule 1: Name the Target Integral I at the Very Start:
Neither eax nor sin bx / cos bx ever terminates when differentiated. Naming I = ∫ eax sin bx dx lets you solve algebraically once I reappears after two rounds!
Rule 2: Stay Consistent Between Pass 1 & Pass 2!
If you choose T (sin bx) as u in Pass 1, you MUST also choose T (cos bx) as u in Pass 2.
Trap: Switching to u = eax in Pass 2 simply reverses Pass 1 and gives the useless identity I = I!
Don't Forget + C After Dividing by the Coefficient of I:
When solving 54I = …, append + C at the end of the final expression for I.
