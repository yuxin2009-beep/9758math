5.5 Lesson 2 • Separable Differential Equations dydx = f(x)g(y)
What is a Variable Separable DE? A first-order differential equation is variable separable if it can be written as a product of a function of x and a function of y:
dydx = f(x)g(y)  (e.g. 2x(y − 1), y cot x)
Sums like dydx = x + y cannot be factored into f(x)g(y) and are not directly separable.
Why Separating Variables Works Dividing both sides by g(y) (for g(y) ≠ 0) and integrating with respect to x applies the Chain Rule in reverse:
∫ 1g(y) dydx dx = ∫ f(x) dx  ⇒  ∫ 1g(y) dy = ∫ f(x) dx
All y-terms sit with dy on the left, and all x-terms sit with dx on the right.
