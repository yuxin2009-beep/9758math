3.2 Lesson 3 • Relationship & Angle Between Two Lines in 3D
Four Possible Relationships Between Two Lines L₁ & L₂ in 3D
9758 Misconception Alert (Two Lines: Relationships & Acute Angle):
  • Acute Angle Between Two Lines Always Requires Modulus: Because a line has two opposite direction vectors (d and −d), the dot product d₁ · d₂ can be negative. The angle between two lines is defined as the acute angle 0° ≤ θ ≤ 90°, so you must write cos θ = |d₁ · d₂| / (|d₁||d₂|)!
  • Never equate the same parameter λ for both lines: Always assign λ to L₁ and μ to L₂. Solving the x and y equations finds where their 2D top-down shadows cross; substituting (λ, μ) into the z-equation tests whether they actually meet at the same height in 3D.
