How to Draw a Scatter Diagram on Paper & GC Workflow
9758 Paper Sketching Checklist (Full Marks Guide) Exam Standard
  1. Draw Clean Axes & Label Variables with Units: Sketch the horizontal and vertical axes, mark the origin O, and label both axes clearly (e.g., x (°C) and y (hundreds of cups)). No grid lines or crowded tick marks are needed on an exam sketch.
  2. Plot Clear Crosses (×) Reflecting the Relative Pattern: Space points accurately to show the overall linear or curved trend without joining them dot-to-dot.
  3. Show Only the First and Last Points on the Axes: Draw neat dotted lines from the first point (smallest x) and last point (largest x) to both axes, and label their numerical values directly on the axes (do not write coordinate pairs beside every dot).
  4. Mark the Centroid (x̄, ȳ) When Asked: Plot (x̄, ȳ) with a distinct symbol (e.g., circled cross) when drawing the regression line through (x̄, ȳ).
1. Turn STAT DIAGNOSTICS ON & Enter Data
Press [MODE] → scroll down to STAT DIAGNOSTICS → select ON. Then press [STAT] → 1: Edit and enter x in L1 and y in L2.
2. Plot Scatter Diagram on GC
Press [2nd] [Y=] (STAT PLOT) → 1: Plot1 → On, select the scatter icon (Xlist: L1, Ylist: L2), then press [ZOOM] → 9: ZoomStat.
3. Find Centroid (x̄, ȳ)
Press [STAT] → CALC → 2: 2-Var Stats (Xlist: L1, Ylist: L2) → Calculate. Read x̄ and scroll down for ȳ.
4. Find Correlation r
Press [STAT] → CALC → 4: LinReg(ax+b) (or 8: LinReg(a+bx)) and read r at the bottom of the screen.
9758 Exam Misconception Alert:
  • Include all 3 keywords when interpreting r: Always state (1) strength (strong/moderate/weak), (2) direction (positive/negative), and (3) the word linear correlation in context between the two named variables.
  • r ≈ 0 does NOT mean "no relationship": It means little or no linear correlation—a strong curved pattern can still give r = 0. Also, correlation does not imply causation!
