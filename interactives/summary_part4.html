1. P&C and Probability

TopicFormulas & Laws
Counting • Unordered selection: $^nC_r$; Ordered arrangement: $^nP_r$
• Identical objects: $\frac{n!}{n_1!n_2!\dots n_k!}$
• Circle (unnumbered): $(n-1)!$; Circle (numbered): $n!$
• Methods: Box, Grouping, Slotting, Complement
Probability Laws • $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$
• Mutually exclusive: $P(A \cap B) = 0$
• Independent: $P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$
• Conditional: $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$

2. Discrete RV & Binomial Distribution

ConceptFormula & TI-84 Plus CE
Discrete RV • $\sum P(X=x) = 1$; $E(X) = \sum x P(X=x)$
• $\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$
• $E(aX+b) = aE(X)+b$; $\text{Var}(aX+b) = a^2\text{Var}(X)$
Binomial
$X \sim \text{B}(n, p)$		 • Conditions: $n$ independent trials, 2 outcomes, constant $p$
• $E(X) = np$, $\text{Var}(X) = np(1-p)$
• GC: [2nd][vars] > binompdf for $P(X=x)$; binomcdf for $P(X \le x)$

3. Normal Distribution, Sampling & CLT

ConceptFormula & TI-84 Plus CE
Normal
$X \sim \text{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$		 • Standardisation: $Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \sim \text{N}(0, 1)$
• $aX \pm bY \sim \text{N}(a\mu_X \pm b\mu_Y,\; a^2\sigma_X^2 + b^2\sigma_Y^2)$
• Sample mean: $\bar{X} \sim \text{N}\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)$
• GC: normalcdf(lower, upper, μ, σ) & invNorm(area, μ, σ)
CLT & Unbiased Estimates • For large $n$ ($n \ge 30$), by CLT, $\bar{X} \sim \text{N}\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)$ approx.
• $\hat{\mu} = \bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n}$; $s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum x^2 - \frac{(\sum x)^2}{n}\right)$

4. Hypothesis Testing & Regression

TopicExam Structure & GC Commands
Hypothesis Test 1. Define $X$ and $\mu$; state $H_0: \mu = \mu_0$ vs $H_1$ (1-tail or 2-tail)
2. State distribution of $\bar{X}$ under $H_0$ (cite CLT if non-normal & large $n$)
3. GC: [stat] > TESTS > 1:Z-Test → compute $p$-value
4. If $p\text{-value} \le \alpha$, reject $H_0$ & conclude in context
Correlation & Regression • Turn on $r$: [mode] > Stat Diagnostics: ON
• Line: [stat] > CALC > 4:LinReg(ax+b); passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$
• Use $y$ on $x$ if $x$ is independent; use $x$ on $y$ if $y$ is independent
