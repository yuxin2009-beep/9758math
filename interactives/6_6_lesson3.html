Evaluating the Reliability of a Regression Estimate

1. Interpolation (Inside Data Range)

  • Definition: Predicting y for an x-value lying within the observed interval [xmin, xmax] (or x for y within [ymin, ymax]).
  • Reliability: Generally reliable provided the scatter diagram confirms a linear trend and |r| is close to 1.

2. Extrapolation (Outside Data Range)

  • Definition: Predicting y for an x-value lying outside the observed range [xmin, xmax].
  • Reliability: Generally unreliable because we cannot assume the linear relationship continues to hold beyond the observed data (physical limits, saturation, or curvature may occur).

4-Point Checklist When Commenting on Reliability in 9758 Exams

  1. Interpolation vs. Extrapolation: State clearly whether the given value lies within or outside the observed range of the data (quote the exact min and max values!).
  2. Strength of Linear Correlation (|r|): Check whether |r| is close to 1 (strong linear fit → small residuals) or weak (wide scatter → large prediction error even for interpolation!).
  3. Linearity & Outliers on Scatter Diagram: Even if |r| is high, verify the scatter diagram shows no obvious curve or influential outlier distorting the line.
  4. Correct Regression Line Choice: Confirm y on x was used to estimate y from x (and x on y to estimate x from y when both are random, unless x is controlled).
9758 Exam Misconception Alert:
  • Interpolation is NOT automatically reliable! If |r| is weak (e.g., r = 0.35 in Scenario 2), points are widely scattered around the line, so even an interpolated estimate is unreliable.
  • High |r| does NOT make extrapolation safe! In Scenario 3, r = 0.996 inside [10 km, 35 km], yet extrapolating to 60 km fails completely because human running pace drops sharply due to exhaustion.
