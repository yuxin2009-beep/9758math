5.5 Lesson 3 • Reducing Differential Equations via a Given Substitution
How to Reduce a Differential Equation Using a Given Substitution
When a differential equation cannot be separated directly into dydx = f(x)g(y), a given substitution connecting u, x, and y transforms it into a separable equation in u and x:
  1. Differentiate the substitution with respect to x (using the Chain Rule or Product Rule) to express dydx in terms of dudx, u, and x.
  2. Substitute for both y and dydx into the original differential equation and simplify until no y remains—obtaining a separable equation in u and x.
  3. Separate variables, integrate for u, and back-substitute the original expression for u in terms of x and y (applying any initial condition to find C).
