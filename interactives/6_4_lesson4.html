Population Variance & Sample Size

Suppose SP Group 4-room HDB monthly electricity consumption has true population mean μ = 360 kWh and true variance σ² = 500 kWh². Compare the long-run average of s² (dividing by n − 1) against biased variance (dividing by n).

True Population Variance σ² σ² = 500
Sample Size n (Small n exaggerates the bias!) n = 5
Why does dividing by n underestimate σ²?
Sample deviations (xᵢ − x̄)² are measured from the sample mean x̄ (which sits closer to the sample points than the true population mean μ). Dividing by n − 1 corrects this downward bias so E(S²) = σ²!
Unbiased s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1) Biased v = Σ(x − x̄)² / n True Target σ²

Across 1,000 samples of size n = 5, the average of s² converges to 500.0 (unbiased!), while dividing by n averages around (4/5) × 500 = 400.0 (systematically too small).
