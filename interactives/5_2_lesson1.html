Part A: First-Principles Derivation of the Maclaurin Formula
Goal: Suppose f(x) = a0 + a1x + a2x² + a3x³ + a4x4 + ⋯ around x = 0. How do we find each coefficient ak?
General Maclaurin Series Formula
f(x) = f(0) + f′(0)x + f″(0)2!x² + f′′′(0)3!x³ + ⋯ + f(k)(0)k!xk + ⋯
Derivation: Equation of Tangent to y = f(x) at x = 0
1. From Coordinate Geometry: At x = 0, the point of contact is (0, f(0)) and the gradient is m = f′(0):
y − y1 = m(x − x1)  ⇒  y − f(0) = f′(0)(x − 0)  ⇒  y = f(0) + f′(0)x
2. From the Maclaurin Series: For very small |x|, neglecting x² and higher powers leaves the linear approximation P1(x):
y = f(0) + f′(0)x  (the first two terms of the Maclaurin expansion!)

Key Exam Principle (9758 H2 Math):
1. A Maclaurin approximation Pk(x) is only valid when x lies inside the interval of convergence.
2. Inside the valid interval, accuracy improves dramatically when (a) |x| is small (closer to x = 0), or (b) more higher-order terms are included.
3. Outside the interval of convergence (e.g. x = 1.4 for ln(1 + x)), adding more terms makes the approximation diverge!
