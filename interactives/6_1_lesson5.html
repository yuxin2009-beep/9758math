1. Equiprobable Sample Space
P(E) = n(E) / n(S) requires every outcome in S to be equally likely (random selection or random seating).
2. Match Counting Frameworks
• Unordered Selection: n(E) and n(S) both use ⁿCᵣ.
• Ordered Arrangement: n(E) and n(S) both use n! / ⁿPᵣ.
3. "At Least" → Casework or Complement
For "at least k", sum disjoint exact cases (k, k+1, …) or compute 1 − P(fewer than k).
9758 Exam Misconception Alert — Mixing ⁿCᵣ and ⁿPᵣ in P(E) = n(E) / n(S):
  • Golden Rule of Consistency: Never put an ordered permutation (ⁿPᵣ or r!) in the numerator if your denominator is an unordered combination (ⁿCᵣ)—and vice versa!
  • Why ⁿCᵣ is usually cleaner for selection problems: Even if items are drawn one by one without replacement, ignoring order in both numerator and denominator gives the exact same probability with far less algebra!
